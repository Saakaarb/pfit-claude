Warnings
--------
1. 47 of 1024 samples (4.6%) sit at exactly y = 10.000, and 52 (5.1%) above
   9.9. That is the level sensor and the tank overflow saturating together, and
   the model being fitted is the benchmark's baseline, which deliberately does
   not represent either. Those samples are therefore unreachable by any
   parameter set: the model can exceed 10, the data cannot. This puts a floor on
   the achievable loss and will bias the fit as it trades peak error against the
   rest of the record. Expect a systematic one-sided residual at every peak, and
   read it as a model-structure gap rather than a fit failure.

2. x1(0) = 5.0 is an assumption, not a measurement. Only x2 is observed
   (D10 confirms init_val 5.205 matches data row 0 exactly), and this is
   regime 1 - the solve starts at the first save point, so solution[0] IS y0
   and any error in x1(0) cannot be absorbed by the parameters. The benchmark
   names "the unknown initial values of the states" as one of its four
   challenges precisely because it must be estimated, which this framework
   cannot do. The cost is an initial transient error decaying with the upper
   tank's own time constant.

3. L3 reports level_lower with 23% of samples in the lowest decile of its range
   and 10% in the highest, i.e. the record spends much of its time against both
   rails rather than in the middle. The upper rail is warning 1; the lower rail
   means a large share of the residual comes from the region where sqrt(x2) is
   least sensitive to k3.

Recommendations
---------------
[R1] integrator: Dopri5 -> Kvaerno5
     Evidence: the Jacobian is lower-triangular,
          J = [[-k1/(2*sqrt(x1)), 0], [k2/(2*sqrt(x1)), -k3/(2*sqrt(x2))]],
          so its eigenvalues are exactly the two diagonal entries - no
          eigenvalue estimation or sampling is needed. Both are of the form
          "the state limits its own rate", and the slavedness test is decisive:
          for x1, f = -k1*sqrt(x1) + k4*u tends to 0 as the tank reaches
          quasi-equilibrium while df/dx1 = -k1/(2*sqrt(x1)) stays at a finite
          nonzero value. The ratio collapses while the derivative does not:
          that is a slaved mode, which is exactly what an implicit method
          converts into larger steps.
          Whether it is FAST enough to matter depends on where in the box you
          are, and the box spans both regimes: at the derived estimate
          k1 ~ 2e-02 the upper tank's timescale is 2*sqrt(5)/k1 = 224 s, slower
          than the data's own tau_min of 34 s (non-stiff); at the top of the box
          k1 = 10 it is 0.45 s, a 75x separation (stiff). The accuracy limit is
          only t_span/tau_min = 4092/34 = 120 steps, while explicit stability at
          the top corner needs 4092 * 2.94 = 1.2e+04 - a 100x gap.
     Why: R1 is explicit that a box which admits the slaved corner at all is the
          "stiffness unknown" case, not a tie, and that case goes to implicit.
          The population search visits the whole box, so with Dopri5 the entire
          high-k1 region would be integrated at the stability limit and much of
          it would exhaust max_steps, scoring error_loss - indistinguishable
          from a genuinely bad parameter set. Implicit on the non-stiff part of
          the box merely costs ~3-10x per step. This overrides the explicit
          choice drafted in the config, which was argued from the physically
          expected parameter values rather than from the bounds.
          With Kvaerno5 the step becomes accuracy-limited, so max_steps = 10000
          needs no change: it is ~80x the 120 steps accuracy requires, leaving
          headroom for candidates that drain a tank toward the sqrt kink.
     Edit: gradient_opt.integrator -> Kvaerno5

[R9] (k1, k4) is a near-flat direction; expect ~3 of 4 parameters constrained
     Evidence: 4 trainable parameters against 1 observed column x 3 = 3, so the
          count trigger fires. Structurally, the substitution k1 -> a*k1,
          k4 -> a*k4 leaves the upper tank's quasi-steady level unchanged, since
          k1*sqrt(x1) = k4*u gives sqrt(x1) = (k4/k1)*u. The only route by which
          x1 reaches the observed x2 is the term k2*sqrt(x1), which is therefore
          also unchanged. All that changes is how quickly x1 settles - and at
          the derived estimate that settling takes 224 s against a record of
          4092 s, so it is nearly invisible in y.
     Why: expect the post-fit sloppiness report to show one near-zero
          eigenvalue whose eigenvector pairs k1 with k4, and expect their
          individual fitted values to be poorly determined while the ratio
          k4/k1 and the products reaching x2 are well determined. The fix is a
          reparameterisation to (k4/k1, k2*k4/k1, k3) - not more iterations,
          since a flat direction has nothing to descend.
