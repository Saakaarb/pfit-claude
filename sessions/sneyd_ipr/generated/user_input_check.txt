[R1] Integrator family: implicit — the current INTEGRATOR = Kvaerno5 is correct
     Evidence: the RHS is smooth — all ten fluxes are rational functions of the
          states and parameters, with no sign(), abs(), clip(), floor() or any
          where() on a state-dependent condition. Stiffness: all 14 rate
          constants share the box [1E-3, 1E5], so a single candidate may carry
          timescales 1/k spanning 8 decades. The data confirms the fast/slow
          split — in ca04.csv the open probability goes 0.008 -> 0.327 inside
          the first sample interval (0 -> 0.07 s) and then decays over the
          remaining 0.91 s, so the rise is entirely unresolved by the 0.07 s
          sampling.
     Why: R1 calls for an implicit solver on a smooth RHS with a wide timescale
          ratio or unknown stiffness. Kvaerno5 appears in the solver table of
          lib/LLM/api/diffrax.md as implicit (stiff) and is marked validated.
          The asymmetry matters here: an implicit method on a candidate that
          happens to be non-stiff costs ~3-10x per step but still converges,
          whereas an explicit method on a candidate with k = 1E5 would need a
          step of order 1E-5 across a 0.98 s span — roughly 1E5 steps, well past
          MAX_STEPS = 10000 — and would score error_loss.
     Edit: none — no change needed.

[R9] Identifiability: expect a sloppy spectrum; this is a property of the model,
     not something to fix by raising the budget
     Evidence: 14 trainable parameters against a SINGLE observed column per
          experiment (open probability), i.e. N = 14 > 3 x 1. The rates are
          entangled in the RHS by construction: they enter only through the
          lumped constants L1 = k_1*l2/(k1*l_2), L3 = k_2*l4/(k2*l_4),
          L5 = k_4*l6/(k4*l_6) — each a ratio, so scaling its numerator and
          denominator together leaves the flux unchanged — and through the fixed
          sums and products (k_1 + l_2) in v3 and v7, and (k1*L1 + l2) in v2 and
          v6.
     Why: those combinations are the identifiable quantities; the individual
          rates are not. Expect several parameters to pin against MIN_VAL or
          MAX_VAL at the optimum and the post-fit sloppiness report to show a
          wide eigenvalue spread. Neither is evidence of a failed fit, and
          neither is fixed by more iterations — the remedy would be fewer
          trainable parameters or a reparameterisation onto (L1, L3, L5) plus
          the sums, which is a change to the model, not to the settings.
     Edit: none — no change needed; read the sloppiness report with this in mind.

[R8] Aggregation across the 9 experiments is an unweighted mean, and one clamp
     condition is represented twice
     Evidence: the XML declares 9 <EXPERIMENT> blocks. Their clamps are
          (IP3, Ca) = (10, 0.1), (10, 0.2), (10, 0.4), (10, 1), (10, 3),
          (10, 10), (3, 0.4), (5, 0.4), (10, 0.4) — ca04.csv and ip10.csv carry
          the SAME clamp (10 uM IP3, 0.4 uM Ca). Their data differ
          (peak Po 0.327 vs 0.337, first row 0.008 vs 0.014), so they are two
          replicates of one condition rather than a duplication error. All nine
          files are otherwise identical in shape: 15 rows, uniform 0.07 s
          spacing over [0, 0.98], no blank cells.
     Why: the framework averages the nine per-experiment losses with no
          weighting available at the XML level, so (10, 0.4) carries 2/9 of the
          objective while every other condition carries 1/9. That is a
          defensible choice — it is the condition with two measurements — but it
          is a choice, and it is being made implicitly. Per-experiment weighting
          would require a library change and is out of scope here.
     Edit: none, unless you want the condition down-weighted, in which case drop
           one of the two <EXPERIMENT> blocks (ca04.csv or ip10.csv).
