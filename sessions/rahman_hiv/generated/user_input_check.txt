[R1] Integrator family: implicit — the current INTEGRATOR = Kvaerno5 is correct
     Evidence: the RHS is smooth — sums, products, one division by the total
          population and one np.exp() for the behavioural-change damping; no
          sign(), abs(), clip() or state-dependent where(). Stiffness is bounded
          numerically rather than guessed, by the Bendixson matrix measure over
          the box (autodiff J = df/dy at sampled states and parameters; no
          trajectory is integrated):
              max |Re lambda|  <=  596.8 / year
          attained at rel_n = 819, beta_m = 0.75. Against the 22-year record,
              N_explicit = 22 x 596.8 = 13,130 steps needed for STABILITY,
          while accuracy needs only t_span / tau_min = 22 / 1.0 = 22 steps
          (tau_min = 1 year, the smallest local timescale in the prevalence
          column).
     Why: the gap between the stability requirement and the accuracy
          requirement is ~600x, and that gap is the definition of stiffness. An
          explicit method would be pinned at ~13,130 steps for the whole solve —
          above the current MAX_STEPS of 10,000 — so it is not merely slower, it
          would fail outright on that part of the box and score error_loss,
          hiding the region from the optimizer. Explicit is therefore NOT
          demonstrably safe here and R1's tie-break does not apply.
     Correcting a reading that a ratio of rate constants would have produced:
          the bare bounds suggest every rate is capped at 1.0/year, which would
          give only ~22 characteristic times and imply a non-stiff system. That
          is wrong, because the transmission rates are products —
          beta_n = rel_n * beta_m with rel_n up to 1000 — so the effective rate
          reaches ~600/year. A rate-constant ratio under-estimates whenever a
          rate reaches the Jacobian multiplied by another fitted parameter,
          which is the unsafe direction; the matrix measure catches it.
     Edit: none — no change needed.

[R9] Identifiability: nine rates against one aggregate observable, and three of
     them are close to invisible by construction
     Evidence: the lone observable is prevalence = (1 - S/N) * 100, which
          depends on the state ONLY through the susceptible fraction. Every
          individual who is not susceptible counts identically towards it,
          whatever compartment they occupy. So the transitions that merely move
          people between non-susceptible compartments — tau_w (Iw -> Tw), g_m
          (Tm -> Tn) and g_w (Tw -> Tm) — leave prevalence unchanged except
          through the difference in stage mortality (mu_Iw = 0.3108 versus
          mu_Tw = 0.1752, and so on), which is a second-order effect on the
          quantity being fitted. The remaining six enter prevalence directly
          through the force of infection, so they are better constrained.
     Why: N = 9 trainable against a single observed column is already three
          times the count R9 flags on, but the structural argument is the
          stronger one and it holds no matter how much data is collected on this
          observable: only stage-resolved measurements can separate the treated
          compartments. Expect a wide sloppiness spectrum and expect tau_w, g_m
          and g_w in particular to be poorly determined. Neither is a fit
          failure, and neither is fixed by a larger budget or a tighter box.
     Edit: none — no change needed; read those three fitted values as "not
           determined by this data".
